## Overview

### Purpose
This document outlines the process for obtaining Institutional Review Board (IRB) approval for collecting real patient data for the NeuroPredict-AI system for early detection of Alzheimer's and Parkinson's diseases.

### Regulatory Framework
- **Primary**: Institutional Review Board (IRB) approval under 45 CFR 46 (Common Rule)
- **Medical Device**: FDA 21 CFR Part 812 (Investigational Device Exemption)
- **Privacy**: HIPAA Authorization under 45 CFR 164.508
- **International**: Declaration of Helsinki (WMA)
- **Data Protection**: GDPR (if applicable in EU)

### Study Classification
- **Type**: Prospective observational study with retrospective data collection
- **Risk Level**: Minimal risk to moderate risk
- **Category**: Human Subjects Research with Protected Health Information (PHI)

## Research Protocol

### Protocol Template

#### 1. Title and Version
```
Protocol Title: Development and Validation of NeuroPredict-AI: 
                A Multi-Modal Artificial Intelligence System for 
                Early Detection of Alzheimer's and Parkinson's Diseases

Protocol Version: 1.0
Protocol Date: [Date]
Amendment Date: [If applicable]
```

#### 2. Abstract
```
Background: Alzheimer's and Parkinson's diseases represent major public 
health challenges with limited early detection capabilities.

Objective: To develop and validate an AI-based system that integrates 
imaging, cognitive, and biomarker data for early disease detection.

Design: Prospective observational study with nested case-control analysis

Population: 1000 participants (age 45-85): 
  - 300 healthy controls
  - 200 mild cognitive impairment (MCI)
  - 250 Alzheimer's disease
  - 250 Parkinson's disease

Duration: 3 years (2025-2028)

Primary Outcome: Diagnostic accuracy (sensitivity, specificity, AUC) 
                 of the AI system compared to clinical diagnosis
```

**Research Questions:**

#### 6. Study Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**

*Healthy Controls:*
- Age 45-85 years
- No cognitive complaints
- MMSE ≥ 28
- No neurological disease history
- Able to undergo MRI

*Alzheimer's Disease:*
- Age 50-85 years
- Clinical diagnosis of probable AD (NIA-AA criteria)
- MMSE 15-26
- MRI compatible

*Parkinson's Disease:*
- Age 45-85 years
- Clinical diagnosis of PD (UK Brain Bank criteria)

**Exclusion Criteria:**

- Severe medical conditions affecting cognition
- Substance abuse history
- Pregnant or nursing
- Unable to provide informed consent

## Data Management Plan

### 1. Data Collection and Storage

#### Data Types
1. **Demographic Data**: Age, sex, race, ethnicity, education
2. **Clinical Data**: Diagnosis, medical history, medications
3. **Imaging Data**: MRI scans (DICOM format)
4. **Cognitive Data**: Test scores, assessments
5. **Biomarker Data**: Lab results
6. **Genetic Data**: APOE status (if collected)

#### Data Collection Methods
- **Electronic Data Capture (EDC)**: REDCap or similar HIPAA-compliant system
- **DICOM PACS**: For medical imaging
- **Laboratory Information System**: For biomarker results
- **Paper Source Documents**: Converted to electronic format

#### Data Storage Infrastructure

**Primary Storage:**
- **Database**: PostgreSQL with encryption at rest (AES-256)
- **File Storage**: HIPAA-compliant cloud storage (AWS/Azure with BAA)
- **Backup**: Daily automated backups, 30-day retention
- **Archive**: Long-term storage for 7+ years post-study

**Security Measures:**
- Encryption at rest (AES-256)
- Encryption in transit (TLS 1.3)
- Multi-factor authentication
- Role-based access control
- Audit logging
- Regular security assessments

### 2. Data De-identification

#### De-identification Strategy

**Direct Identifiers to Remove:**
1. Names
2. Addresses (geographic subdivisions smaller than state)
3. Dates (except year) - shift all dates by random offset
4. Phone numbers, fax numbers, email addresses
5. Social Security numbers
6. Medical record numbers
7. Health plan beneficiary numbers
8. Account numbers
9. Certificate/license numbers
10. Vehicle identifiers and serial numbers
11. Device identifiers and serial numbers
12. URLs
13. IP addresses
14. Biometric identifiers
15. Full-face photographs
16. Any other unique identifying number or code

**HIPAA Safe Harbor Method:**
- Remove all 18 HIPAA identifiers
- Verify no remaining information could identify individual
- Document de-identification process

**Limited Data Set (if needed):**
- May retain dates, city, state, ZIP code
- Requires data use agreement
- Stricter access controls

#### De-identification Process
1. Export data from secure database
2. Apply de-identification script (automated)
3. Manual review for residual identifiers
4. Quality control check
5. Create de-identified dataset
6. Maintain linkage key in separate secure location

### 3. Data Quality Management

#### Quality Control Procedures

**Data Entry:**
- Double data entry for critical variables
- Range and logic checks
- Real-time validation rules
- Query management system

**Data Monitoring:**
- Regular data quality reports
- Missing data tracking
- Consistency checks across time points
- Source document verification (sample-based)

**Data Cleaning:**
- Documented cleaning procedures
- Query resolution
- Outlier investigation
- Final database lock

### 4. Data Sharing and Access

#### Internal Access

**Access Levels:**
- **Level 1 (Full Access)**: PI and data manager
- **Level 2 (Analysis Access)**: Statisticians, AI developers (de-identified data)
- **Level 3 (Limited Access)**: Clinical staff (only assigned participants)
- **Level 4 (Read-Only)**: IRB, monitors, auditors

**Access Controls:**
- Unique user IDs
- Strong passwords (complexity requirements)
- Multi-factor authentication for remote access
- Automatic timeout after inactivity
- Access logs maintained

#### External Data Sharing

**Data Sharing Policy:**
- De-identified data may be shared with qualified researchers
- Requires:
  1. IRB approval (or exemption determination)
  2. Data use agreement
  3. Written request with research plan
  4. PI approval

**Public Data Repository:**
- De-identified dataset deposited in [repository name]
- After primary publications
- With data dictionary and code

### 5. Data Retention and Disposal

#### Retention Period
- **Regulatory Minimum**: 7 years after study completion (FDA requirement)
- **Institutional Policy**: [Check institution policy]
- **Extended Retention**: For long-term follow-up or secondary analyses

#### Data Disposal
- **Electronic Data**: Secure deletion (DoD 5220.22-M standard)
- **Physical Documents**: Shredding or incineration
- **Backup Media**: Secure destruction
- **Documentation**: Certificate of destruction maintained

### 6. Data Breach Response Plan

#### Prevention Measures
- Encryption, access controls, monitoring
- Security training for all staff
- Regular security audits
- Incident detection systems

#### Response Procedures

**If Breach Suspected:**
1. **Immediate Actions**:
   - Contain the breach
   - Secure systems
   - Document incident
   - Notify PI and Data Security Officer

2. **Assessment** (within 24 hours):
   - Determine scope and severity
   - Identify affected individuals
   - Assess risk to participants

3. **Notification**:
   - IRB: Within 5 business days
   - Participants: Within 60 days (if harm possible)
   - Regulatory authorities: As required (e.g., HHS if HIPAA breach >500 individuals)
   - Law enforcement: If criminal activity

4. **Remediation**:
   - Fix vulnerabilities
   - Implement additional safeguards
   - Retrain staff if needed
   - Update procedures

5. **Documentation**:
   - Incident report
   - Investigation findings
   - Corrective actions
   - Lessons learned

### Mitigation Strategies

#### Technical Controls
1. **Encryption**: AES-256 at rest, TLS 1.3 in transit
2. **Access Control**: RBAC, MFA, least privilege
3. **Monitoring**: SIEM, audit logs, intrusion detection
4. **Backups**: Daily automated, tested recovery
5. **Vulnerability Management**: Regular scanning, patching

## Ethical Considerations

### Ethical Principles

#### 1. Respect for Persons

**Autonomy:**
- Voluntary participation
- Informed consent process
- Right to withdraw
- No coercion or undue influence

**Protection of Vulnerable Populations:**
- Cognitive impairment: Assess capacity to consent
- Surrogate consent allowed if appropriate
- Ongoing assent even with surrogate consent
- Special protections for decisionally impaired

#### 2. Beneficence

**Maximize Benefits:**
- Scientific knowledge generation
- Potential for improved diagnostics
- Participant feedback (appropriate results)

**Minimize Harms:**
- Minimize risks through design and monitoring
- Risk mitigation strategies
- Adverse event management
- Psychological support available

#### 3. Justice

**Fair Subject Selection:**
- Inclusion of diverse populations
- No exploitation of vulnerable groups
- Equitable access to benefits
- Avoid over-research of convenient populations

**Equitable Distribution:**
- Recruitment across demographics
- Translation of materials if needed
- Compensation for all participants
- Access to study results

### Specific Ethical Issues

#### 1. Incidental Findings

**Definition**: Findings discovered during research that may be clinically significant but are outside the scope of the study.

**Management Plan:**

**Imaging Findings:**
- All MRI scans reviewed by qualified radiologist
- Clinically significant findings communicated to participant and physician
- Referral for clinical follow-up provided
- Process explained in informed consent

**Biomarker Findings:**
- Return results that are clinically actionable
- Do not return results of uncertain significance
- Provide genetic counseling for genetic findings
- Process explained in informed consent

**Communication Protocol:**
1. Finding identified by qualified professional
2. Clinical significance assessed
3. If significant: participant contacted, results explained
4. Referral provided for follow-up
5. Documented in study records

#### 2. Return of Research Results

**Individual Results:**

*Will Return:*
- MRI scan and radiologist report
- Cognitive test summary
- Clinically actionable biomarker results

*Will NOT Return:*
- Research-grade AI predictions (not clinically validated)
- Results of uncertain clinical significance
- Aggregate research findings (instead offered in summary form)

*Process:*
- Results accompanied by explanation
- Reviewed with participant (in-person or phone)
- Provided to participant's physician with permission
- Resources for questions/follow-up

**Aggregate Results:**
- Summary of study findings provided to all participants
- Publication links sent to interested participants
- Results presented at participant events (if interest)

#### 3. Therapeutic Misconception

**Risk**: Participants may believe they are receiving clinical care or that the AI system is clinically validated.

**Mitigation:**
- Clear explanation that this is research
- AI system is not validated for clinical use
- Study does not provide diagnosis or treatment
- Emphasize research purpose in all communications
- Assessment of understanding during consent

#### 4. Genetic Information

**If Collecting APOE Status:**

**Special Considerations:**
- Genetic information is sensitive and permanent
- Risk of genetic discrimination (mitigated by GINA, ACA)
- Implications for family members
- No current medical intervention for APOE status

**Additional Consent Elements:**
- Separate consent for genetic testing
- Option to decline
- Genetic counseling available
- Right not to know results
- Communication with family members

#### 5. AI and Machine Learning Ethics

**Bias and Fairness:**
- Ensure diverse training data
- Assess performance across demographic groups
- Monitor for algorithmic bias
- Transparent reporting of performance by subgroup

**Explainability:**
- Provide explanations for AI predictions
- Feature importance analysis
- Clinician review and override capability

**Validation:**
- Rigorous validation before clinical use
- External validation datasets
- Ongoing performance monitoring

**Human Oversight:**
- AI as decision support, not replacement
- Clinician review of predictions
- Override capability

#### 6. Data Sharing and Secondary Use

**Ethical Data Sharing:**
- De-identification to protect privacy
- Data use agreements required
- IRB review for secondary use
- Participant consent for future use (optional)
- Certificate of Confidentiality protections

**Commercialization:**
- Potential for commercial product development disclosed
- Participants do not receive financial benefit
- Ethical considerations of profit from participant data
